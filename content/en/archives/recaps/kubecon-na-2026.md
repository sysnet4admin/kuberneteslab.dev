KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026 ran November 9-12 at the Salt Palace
Convention Center in Salt Lake City. I attended with CNCF travel funding again, and
besides my talk I picked up several jobs helping the event run as a CNCF Ambassador.

On Tuesday, Tsomorlig Khishigbold brought me to the
[Women's Community Gathering](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1336486).
Everyone took a turn sharing their story, and I finally met Niki Manoledaki in
person and heard about the work she has been doing.

![Women's Community Gathering](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/02-womens-gathering.jpg "Women's Community Gathering (photo: CNCF Flickr)")

Before my talk I stopped by the
[SIG ContribEx (Contributor Experience) session](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1289639).
Kaslin Fields, Natalie Fisher, Priyanka Saggu, and Nabarun Pal walked through the
sub-project and governance updates, which gave me a clear picture of where the
Kubernetes contributor community is heading.

![SIG ContribEx session](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/03-contribex.jpg "SIG ContribEx session")

Tuesday ended with my talk on the Operations + Performance track, "How Far Are
Open-Source LLMs from Running Real Kubernetes Operations? A 10-Scenario Benchmark."
I gave open-source LLMs 10 Kubernetes operations scenarios, measured how far each
one got, and compared them with commercial models. The
[slides](https://github.com/sysnet4admin/talks/tree/main/KubeCon/2026-NA) are in the
repository. Someone asked why Anthropic's Claude was not among the commercial
models. The honest answer: at the time, the only API credits I could get were for
OpenAI's Codex. There was no other reason (really...).

![On stage](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/04-my-talk.jpg "Talk on the Operations + Performance track")

Wednesday morning was the best part of the week for me. I spent most of it at the
[TCG (Technical Community Group) Students kiosk](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1373666)
in the Project Pavilion with Emily Chen, talking with students.

![TCG Students kiosk](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/05-tcg-kiosk.jpg "At the TCG Students kiosk with Emily Chen")

At 10:40 I stepped away for 20 minutes to guide a
[Project Pavilion tour](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1341037).
I carried the tour sign and walked the group from kiosk to kiosk, while Rafael
Brito, who was paired with me, went ahead to check that the next kiosk could take
the tour. Without Rafael the tour would not have gone as well as it did.

![Project Pavilion tour](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/06-pavilion-tour.jpg "Guiding the Project Pavilion tour")

Thanks also to YoungJoon Jeong and Seol Heo for stopping by the Pavilion that day.
I am sorry I missed their talk because of jet lag and another commitment.

In the afternoon I helped at two Contribfests as a CNCF Ambassador. A Contribfest
puts maintainers and contributors in one room to make real contributions. A helper
does not run the session; the job is to assist the maintainers with whatever they
need and help attendees when they get stuck. First was
[Kyverno](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1294761),
run by Jim Bugwadia, Cortney Nickerson, and Saurabh Garg, with about 40 people.

![Kyverno Contribfest](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/07-contribfest-kyverno.jpg "Helping an attendee at the Kyverno Contribfest")

Right after, in the same room, came
[Inspektor Gadget](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1294763),
run by Qasim Sarfraz and Michael Friese. The goal was to publish a first gadget,
and I helped about 20 attendees create a repository from the GitHub template and
build the top_syscall gadget with GitHub Actions.

![Inspektor Gadget Contribfest](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/08-contribfest-gadget.jpg "Inspektor Gadget Contribfest")

After the Contribfests, thanks to Samudra Weerasinghe and Savi Saluwadana, I joined
the OpenChoreo dinner hosted by WSO2 that evening.

On Thursday afternoon I was a mentor at
[Peer Group Mentoring](https://kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america-2026.sessionize.com/session/1339180)
again, as I was at KubeCon Japan 2026. My table was on the technical track, and the
topics included the engineer's role in the era of AI agents, working with SRE
teams whose priorities differ from yours, and best practices for infrastructure.
The topic we spent the most time on, though, was how to lead your own career path
and keep growing. It was a very meaningful hour for me too.

![Peer Group Mentoring](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/09-peer-mentoring.jpg "Peer Group Mentoring (photo: CNCF Flickr)")

I also came home with gifts: a Texas sticker and Kubernetes earrings from Mars
Toktonaliev, a shampoo bar and a panda bookmark from Emily, a Tux and Kubernetes pin
from LF Education, Indian craft chocolate from Priyanka, and a candle from the
Women's Community Gathering.

![Gifts](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/10-gifts.jpg "Gifts from this KubeCon")

Finally, thanks to Audra Montenegro, the CNCF team, and the fellow ambassadors I
worked with. On Tuesday morning the ambassadors and Kubestronauts met for the
Ambassador + Kubestronaut Breakfast. Bringing people from this many countries into
one room is something only KubeCon makes possible.

![CNCF Ambassadors and Kubestronauts](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/11-ambassadors-group.jpg "Ambassador + Kubestronaut Breakfast group photo")

After mentoring on Thursday I stopped by Temple Square. The next morning I caught
the first train to the airport, and this KubeCon was over.

![Temple Square](/images/recaps/kubecon-na-2026/12-temple-square.jpg "Temple Square, Salt Lake City")
