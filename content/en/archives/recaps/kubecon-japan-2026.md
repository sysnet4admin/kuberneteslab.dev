KubeCon + CloudNativeCon Japan 2026 ran July 28-30 at PACIFICO Yokohama. I
attended on a CNCF scholarship (travel funding). At one point they called the
scholarship recipients up on stage, and the photo happened on the spur of the
moment. There were surely more recipients than the few of us who were in the room
right then, and you can see on our faces that nobody was ready for it.

![Scholarship recipients](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/03-scholarship-recipients.jpg "On stage with the other scholarship recipients")

On the Novice track I presented "I Tested 7 So You Only Need 1: Your First Gateway
API Migration in 5 Minutes." Based on testing seven Gateway API implementations
under identical conditions, the talk covered where to start if this is your first
migration. The
[slides](https://github.com/sysnet4admin/talks/tree/main/KubeCon/2026-Japan) and
[demo](https://github.com/sysnet4admin/talks/tree/main/KubeCon/2026-Japan/DEMO)
are in the repository.

On Wednesday I emceed both Lightning Talk blocks in room 313+314.
[Project Lightning Talks](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-japan/program/schedule/?all-sessions=project-opportunities&date=wed-jul-29&engage-with-projects=project-lightning-talks)
ran 11:30 to 12:40 with nine CNCF projects, five minutes each: Argo CD, k0s,
OpenTelemetry, CoHDI, Kairos, Longhorn, Spiderpool, youki, and Lima. Main Event
Lightning Talks followed at 14:50 with four community stories, also five minutes
each. Thirteen speakers in total.

Keeping that pace while giving every speaker their moment was harder than my own
talk, and I would do it again tomorrow.

![At the mic](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/02-emcee.jpg "Emceeing the Lightning Talks")

On Thursday afternoon I joined
[Peer Group Mentoring](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-japan/program/schedule/?id=1273972)
as a mentor, in room 413 from 14:10 to 15:20. One table, a few chairs, no slides,
just people asking real questions about where to start and what they were stuck
on. I came out of it having learned as much as I gave. If you are on the fence
about signing up next time, sign up.

![Peer Group Mentoring in progress](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/04-peer-mentoring.jpg "During Peer Group Mentoring")

I flew home heavier in gifts than in swag. Snacks from Bangkok, a tea tin from
London, pastries from Shanghai, and a crocheted Cilium bee that now lives on my
desk. Four countries in one bag.

![Gifts](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/05-gifts.jpg "Four countries in one bag")

Yuichi Nakamura, one of the KubeCon Japan organizers, gave me a signed copy of his
own book, 『OSSビジネス活用の教科書』(Nikkei BP), a textbook on putting open source
to work in business. Coming from someone who has spent years on open source at
Hitachi, that signature means a lot. My Japanese is not there yet, so I will start
with the figures and work my way in.

![The signed book](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/06-signed-book.jpg "The signed copy of 『OSSビジネス活用の教科書』(Nikkei BP)")

Thanks to the organizers as well. Everything ran on time, and the rooms were set up
so people would actually talk to each other. None of that happens by accident.
Getting CNCF Ambassadors from this many countries into one room is another thing
only an event like this makes possible.

![CNCF Ambassadors](/images/recaps/kubecon-japan-2026/07-ambassadors.jpg "CNCF Ambassadors gathered at KubeCon Japan")

After every KubeCon the feeling lingers for weeks. This one is no different.
